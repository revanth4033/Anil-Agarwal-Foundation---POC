AAF WEBSITE PROJECT PROJECT CONTEXT AND VISUAL DESIGN BRIEF Version 1.1
| 16 November 2026

PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT This document gives Claude Code the background
and approved visual-design direction for the Anil Agarwal Foundation
(AAF) homepage project. Read this before making design or implementation
decisions.

The task is to design and build the AAF HOMEPAGE ONLY. The homepage
section structure, section order, content requirements and user journey
have already been defined by the UX designer. Do not invent, remove,
reorder or restructure homepage sections. The UX-approved wireframe will
be provided separately and is the source of truth for homepage
information architecture.

The design and implementation focus is visual quality: a polished,
premium, modern, human-centred UI that feels credible for a large
foundation and engaging for the communities and people whose lives its
work affects.

1.  PROJECT BACKGROUND

1.1 Parent organisation: Vedanta Limited Reference homepage:
https://www.vedantalimited.com/eng/index.php

Vedanta Limited describes itself as a leading global producer of metals,
critical minerals and technology. Its operations span India and
international markets. Its business and corporate website communicates
industrial scale, business operations, leadership, sustainability,
technology, investors and social impact.

The parent website’s current navigation includes areas such as: - Who We
Are - Our Businesses - ESG - Technology - Partners - Careers - Investor
Relations - Social Impact - Media - Global Presence

The current homepage presents Vedanta’s businesses and corporate
positioning, and also has a Social Impact area that links to initiatives
including Anil Agarwal Foundation, Nand Ghar, TACO, CSR at Businesses,
Balco Medical, Vedanta Sports and Vedanta Foundation.

Vedanta’s business identity and AAF’s social-impact identity are
related, but they are not the same. Do not make the AAF website look
like an industrial or investor-relations website.

1.2 AAF reference page on the Vedanta website Reference page:
https://www.vedantalimited.com/eng/social_impact_aaf.php

The page describes Anil Agarwal Foundation as: “The umbrella entity for
Vedanta’s social impact initiatives, aiming to create strong, inclusive
& resilient communities in India.”

Published vision: “Empowering communities, transforming lives, and
facilitating nation-building through sustainable and inclusive growth.”

Published mission: “Upholding the highest ethical standards while
implementing projects that foster strong connections with grassroots

The page states that AAF has initiated a plan to invest INR 5,000 crores
over five years in social-impact projects, with focus areas including
healthcare, nutrition, women and child development, animal welfare and
grassroots sports.

Important: these statements and figures are content from the existing
reference page. Before using them as live homepage claims, confirm with
the client that they remain approved and current. Do not silently
update, reinterpret or invent figures.

1.3 What AAF does AAF is presented as the umbrella entity for Vedanta’s
social-impact initiatives. Its purpose is to support strong, inclusive
and resilient communities in India through social-development programmes
and initiatives.

The existing AAF page includes impact categories such as: - Healthcare -
Community infrastructure - Women empowerment - Sports and culture -
Children’s well-being and education - Livelihoods - Environment

The current page highlights projects and initiatives including: - Nand
Ghar: described as AAF’s flagship social-impact project, focused on
early childhood education, nutrition, access to healthcare and women’s
skill development. The page states that 10,000+ Nand Ghars have been set

animal-welfare initiative focused on a sustainable ecosystem for animal

initiatives may be referenced in the broader ecosystem. Do not assume

The new AAF website is a dedicated digital presence for the foundation.
It should communicate the foundation’s purpose, work, credibility and
human impact in its own visual voice while retaining a clear
relationship with Vedanta.

1.4 Founder context Anil Agarwal is the founder associated with Vedanta
and the namesake of the foundation. The foundation’s story is connected
to a philanthropic and social-development vision. Use only
client-approved biography, quotes and claims on the website. Do not
invent founder quotes or attribute general philanthropic statements to
him without a verified source.

2.  PROJECT SCOPE AND WORKING RULES

IN SCOPE: - Design and build the homepage only. - Apply the approved UX
designer’s homepage structure. - Develop a consistent visual system and
implement it in the homepage. - Create polished responsive UI for the
approved homepage sections. - Use real, approved content and credible
imagery.

OUT OF SCOPE UNLESS EXPLICITLY REQUESTED: - Redesigning the full website
or inventing inner-page structures. - Changing the UX designer’s section
order or information architecture. - Adding new sections just because
they seem visually useful. - Rewriting approved copy or inventing impact
claims. - Creating a new logo or replacing the AAF logo. - Treating
proposed design tokens as official brand guidelines.

The UX wireframe and client-approved content take precedence over any
assumptions in this document. When the wireframe is supplied, follow it
closely. Improve visual composition, not the underlying content
structure or user journey.

4.  APPROVED VISUAL DIRECTION

The agreed direction is a combination of:

A. PREMIUM EDITORIAL - Refined typography and clear hierarchy. -
Generous but intentional whitespace. - Strong editorial image
compositions. - Elegant, considered layouts. - A mature, premium visual
feel.

B. MODERN IMPACT - Clear and engaging visual storytelling. - Structured
grids and modular content layouts. - Meaningful impact statistics where
supplied and approved. - Contemporary UI components. - Dynamic but
controlled image and content compositions.

The combined direction should feel: - Premium, but not luxurious or
inaccessible. - Modern, but not trendy or decorative for its own sake. -
Human, warm and inspiring. - Credible and institutional. - Structured,
clean and easy to scan.

Avoid a generic NGO template, an industrial corporate website aesthetic,
an overly playful startup look, or an excessively decorative editorial
magazine treatment.

5.  COLOUR SYSTEM

The selected colour balance is BALANCED.

Use AAF blue and green as the primary brand colours. Use warm neutral
backgrounds and restrained supporting colours to create a calm,
sophisticated interface.

Working primary colours (updated 16 November 2026): - AAF Primary
Blue: #0664A8 - AAF Primary Green: #75C044

Proposed supporting colours (design proposals, not official brand
colours): - Warm Off-white: #F7F8F4 - White: #FFFFFF - Soft Sage:
#EAF1E3 - Pale Blue: #E8EEF4 - Deep Navy: #183D5B - Charcoal: #263238 -
Warm Neutral: #F0EFE9 - Soft Border: #DCE2E7

Suggested usage: - Use white and warm off-white as the dominant page
surfaces. - Use blue for primary brand anchors, links, selected headings
and primary actions. - Use green as a complementary brand accent for
selected highlights, small labels, visual markers and occasional calls
to action. - Use navy and charcoal for readable text and deeper
contrast. - Use pale blue and sage for occasional supporting surfaces,
not every card or section. - Keep colour use restrained and
consistent. - Do not create random gradients or introduce many unrelated
colours. - Do not make every section a large blue or green block.

An earlier visual exploration suggested an approximate balance of 70%
light neutral surfaces, 15% blue, 10% green and 5% deep accents. This is
only a visual starting point, not a strict measured rule. Adjust
naturally to the actual wireframe and content.

Accessibility: - Check text contrast, especially white text on the logo
green. - Do not use the light green as small text on white. - Use
sufficiently dark text for body copy. - Do not communicate status or
meaning through colour alone.

Measured contrast for the working colours (WCAG 2.x): - White on AAF
Blue #0664A8: 6.18:1 (passes AA for all text). - AAF Blue on White /
Off-white / Sage / Pale Blue / Warm Neutral: 5.29–6.18:1 (passes AA). -
AAF Green #75C044 on White: 2.24:1. FAILS for text and is below the 3:1
needed for essential UI graphics. Use green only for decorative accents,
or with Deep Navy or Charcoal text on top of it. - Deep Navy on AAF
Green: 5.05:1; Charcoal on AAF Green: 5.88:1 (pass AA). - Charcoal on
White: 13.16:1; Deep Navy on White: 11.31:1.

6.  TYPOGRAPHY

APPROVED FONT PAIRING: - Lora: major editorial/display headlines. -
Manrope: body text, navigation, labels, buttons, statistics and UI.

The typography direction is RESTRAINED, not excessively expressive.

Guidelines: - Use Lora selectively for key hero and section headlines. -
Use Manrope for all interface and supporting text. - Keep headline line
lengths controlled. - Avoid too many weights and sizes. - Use clear,
comfortable line-height and readable paragraph widths. - Do not use Lora
for every heading or body paragraph. - Load fonts reliably from the
project’s chosen font source and provide sensible fallbacks.

Initial desktop type scale to test (not final until applied to the
wireframe): - H1: Lora, approximately 56px, regular. - H2: Lora,
approximately 40px, regular. - H3: Manrope, approximately 24px,
semibold. - Body: Manrope, approximately 16px, regular. - Small labels:
Manrope, approximately 12–14px, semibold.

Responsive sizes must scale appropriately on tablet and mobile. Avoid
oversized headings that create awkward wrapping.

7.  LAYOUT AND SPACING

The agreed UI direction is spacious, structured and editorial.

Initial desktop specifications: - Main content container: approximately
1280px to 1320px, subject to viewport and wireframe. - Grid: 12-column
desktop grid. - Section vertical spacing: approximately 100px to 120px
as a starting point. - Card radius: approximately 12px to 16px. - Button
radius: approximately 6px to 8px. - Borders: subtle 1px neutral
borders. - Shadows: minimal and only when they improve separation.

Guidelines: - Align content consistently to a shared container. - Use a
deliberate spacing scale rather than arbitrary margins. - Maintain clear
visual hierarchy between sections. - Avoid dense, text-heavy blocks. -
Use asymmetry only when it improves storytelling and does not damage
alignment. - Ensure the layout adapts cleanly to smaller screens. - Do
not force every section into the same card grid. Use visual variety
while retaining a consistent design system.

8.  CARD AND COMPONENT STYLE

The selected card direction is STRUCTURED.

Preferred treatment: - Clear content hierarchy. - Consistent internal
padding. - Subtle borders or quiet surface backgrounds. - Consistent
corner radius. - Strong, well-cropped imagery where relevant. - Minimal
or no heavy shadows. - Clear titles, concise descriptions and purposeful
links. - Consistent hover and focus states.

Useful card patterns may include: - Image-led initiative cards. -
Structured content cards. - Impact/statistic cards. - Story or editorial
cards.

Do not use excessive floating cards, glassmorphism, heavy shadows, thick
borders, unnecessary icon decoration or inconsistent radii.

9.  BUTTONS AND INTERACTION STYLE

Primary button: - AAF blue background. - White readable label. - Clear
hover and focus state. - Modest corner radius. - Concise action label.

Secondary button: - White or transparent background. - AAF blue border
and text. - Consistent dimensions and interaction states.

Text link: - AAF blue. - Clear hover/focus treatment. - Arrow icon only
when it helps communicate navigation.

Motion: - Use subtle transitions and purposeful micro-interactions. -
Keep motion smooth and restrained. - Avoid excessive parallax, bouncing,
constant animation or distracting effects. - Respect reduced-motion
preferences. - Ensure keyboard focus states are visible.

10. PHOTOGRAPHY AND IMAGE DIRECTION

Photography is a major part of the AAF experience. It should communicate
real people, real communities and meaningful outcomes.

Preferred image qualities: - Authentic documentary-style photography. -
Natural light and realistic skin tones. - Genuine expressions and candid
moments. - Respectful representation of beneficiaries. - Realistic
Indian community, rural and programme contexts where appropriate. -
Warm, balanced colour grading. - High-resolution images with intentional
crops. - Consistent visual quality across the homepage.

Potential imagery themes, only where relevant to the approved
wireframe: - Children learning and participating in early childhood
development. - Women building skills and livelihoods. - Healthcare
outreach and community health. - Rural communities and infrastructure. -
Animal care and rehabilitation. - Grassroots sports and young
athletes. - Environmental and livelihood initiatives.

Avoid: - Generic corporate stock photos. - Artificially staged
handshakes or boardroom imagery. - Images that look like unrelated stock
campaigns. - Overly saturated or heavily filtered images. - Repeated use
of the same photograph. - Images that misrepresent the actual programme
or beneficiary group. - Decorative illustrations that replace meaningful
photography without a clear reason.

Image sourcing and integrity: - Prefer client-provided or official
AAF/Vedanta programme imagery. - If using stock or generated imagery
during development, treat it as a placeholder unless approved. - Do not
present generated or stock imagery as documentary evidence of an actual
AAF programme. - Do not invent image captions, locations, beneficiary
identities or programme outcomes. - Use consistent aspect ratios and
intentional object positioning. - Add meaningful alt text for
informative images and empty alt text for purely decorative images.

11. CONTENT AND FACTUAL ACCURACY

The website must communicate AAF accurately and respectfully.

Important rules: - Use the UX designer’s approved content and
client-provided copy as the source of truth. - Do not invent impact
statistics, beneficiaries, locations, programme counts, awards,
testimonials or quotes. - Do not assume old website figures are
current. - If a statistic has no approved source or reporting period,
flag it for confirmation rather than publishing it. - Preserve the
distinction between AAF and the broader Vedanta social-impact
ecosystem. - Do not attribute every Vedanta CSR initiative to AAF unless
the source explicitly does so. - Do not invent legal, governance or
organisational relationships. - Do not fabricate founder quotes or
biographical details. - Use placeholders clearly in development when
content is not yet confirmed.

This document describes design intent. It does not prescribe a
technology stack. First inspect the existing project files and
understand the current framework, dependencies, routes, assets and
conventions before changing anything.

Claude Code should: 1. Read this PROJECT_CONTEXT.md completely. 2.
Inspect the existing project and report its current structure before
implementation. 3. Wait for and inspect the UX-approved homepage
wireframe and content. 4. Map the approved wireframe sections to visual
components without changing section order or information architecture.
5. Establish reusable design tokens for colours, typography, spacing,
radii, borders and shadows. 6. Build reusable components where
appropriate. 7. Implement responsive layouts for desktop, tablet and
mobile. 8. Use the provided AAF logo asset. Do not redraw or alter it.
9. Use the agreed Lora + Manrope typography pairing. 10. Keep components
accessible, semantic and maintainable. 11. Verify layouts at common
desktop and mobile viewport sizes. 12. Check image loading, overflow,
typography wrapping, contrast, keyboard focus and reduced-motion
behaviour. 13. Avoid unnecessary dependencies and avoid rewriting
unrelated project files. 14. Do not make up missing content or imagery.
Flag missing assets and content for the user. 15. Before making major
deviations from the wireframe or this visual direction, ask for
approval.

Do not begin designing an alternative homepage structure. Do not create
extra homepage sections. The next design input will be the UX-approved
wireframe.
